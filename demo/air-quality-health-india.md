# Air Quality, Development and Public Health in India: A 2015 Baseline

## Abstract

This is a demonstration paper written to show how Live Research Paper works. It is not peer-reviewed research and its 2017 publication year is part of the demonstration. It describes a 2015 baseline for India across air quality, population health and development. Its national figures were copied from public datasets so that later observations can be compared with them.

## Introduction

Air quality, health and development are usually reported in separate series with different release calendars.[^1] A paper that fixes them at one moment becomes dated as soon as any one of those series moves. This demonstration asks which of its statements can be revisited with later data, and which cannot.

## Methods

We compiled national annual values for 2015 and compared a small number of them with values for 2000. No survey was conducted and no statistical model was fitted. Figures in this text are rounded.

## Demographic context

India had a population of 1.33 billion people in 2015. The urban population in India was 32.5% of the total population in 2015.

## Population health

Life expectancy at birth in India was 69.3 years in 2015. Female life expectancy at birth in India was 70.9 years in 2015.

The infant mortality rate in India was 36.3 deaths per 1,000 live births in 2015, while the under-5 mortality rate was 43.7 per 1,000 live births in 2015.

Life expectancy at birth in India increased by 6.6 years between 2000 and 2015. The under-5 mortality rate in India fell by 52% between 2000 and 2015.

## Air quality and climate

India's population-weighted mean annual PM2.5 exposure was 51.4 micrograms per cubic metre in 2015. The 2021 WHO air quality guideline for annual mean PM2.5 is 5 micrograms per cubic metre.

For illustration only, suppose that annual mean PM2.5 in Delhi was 100 micrograms per cubic metre in 2015. A city value such as this cannot be refreshed from a national series.

Carbon dioxide emissions per capita in India were 1.7 tonnes in 2015.

## Development indicators

GDP per capita in India was US$1,584 in current US dollars in 2015. Access to electricity in India reached 88.0% of the population in 2015. The share of individuals using the Internet in India was 14.9% in 2015. Cereal yield in India averaged 2,852 kg per hectare in 2015.

As an illustrative placeholder with no matching dataset, assume that 40% of rural households owned a household air monitor.

## Limitations

National means conceal large differences between states and cities. Later observations of these indicators do not test or validate any claim about cause and effect, and this demonstration makes none.

## Conclusion

In an illustrative scenario, the urban share is projected to reach 40% by 2030. A later observation can sit beside that projection but cannot confirm or replace it before 2030.
